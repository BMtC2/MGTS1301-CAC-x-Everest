One · The shape of good AI use

CAC, walked through one summit decision

Co-intelligence (Cognition, AI, Cognition) is an ascent. The thinking that matters happens before and after the AI step, not inside it. Here it is, traced through one Everest moment: the call to push for the summit as the weather window closes.

Base camp · 5,300m

Cognition · think first, alone

Human only

Before any tool, the student forms their own read: who held what information, who pushed to climb, who hesitated, and why.

In the Everest assessment "The Marathoner pushed to summit while I, holding the weather data as Leader, stayed silent. I need to work out why I didn't speak before I ask anything."
2
The ascent · engaging the tool

AI · engage, with your thinking in hand

AI assists

Now AI earns its place: clarifying a theory, testing whether an argument holds, surfacing a counter-view. It speeds the climb; it does not choose the route.

A legitimate ask "Here's my read of why I stayed silent. Does it fit transformational or transactional leadership, and what would each predict I should have done?"
3
Summit · 8,848m

Cognition · evaluate, integrate, own

Human only

The student weighs what AI returned against what actually happened, keeps what holds, rejects what doesn't, and writes in their own voice. The judgment is theirs to stand behind.

The work that stays human "The framing helped, but it missed the information asymmetry that really drove my silence. That part is mine, and it's what the assessment is testing."

The part that won't change

The models get stronger. The catch doesn't go away.

Opus 4.8 Max Gemini 3.1 Pro GPT 5.5 Thinking → and whatever comes next

These models are already formidable, and their successors will be sharper still. But there's a catch more capability doesn't fix: when you bring a flawed assumption, they tend to follow it, confidently, all the way down the hill. It isn't a bug they'll outgrow. It comes from how they're trained to agree with you.

The stronger the model, the more fluent and persuasive that wrong answer becomes, and the harder it is to catch, unless you did your own thinking first.

So your judgment isn't the part AI makes obsolete. It's the one thing that stays yours, and the thing that will set you apart at work.
